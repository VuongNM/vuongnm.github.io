Experiment time! Please put your lab coat on, protective glasses are a must. This is a dangerous experiment and the result will blow you away!



Experiment starts at 8:00 AM. Everybody looks tense.

Vuong takes a coin out of his tight jeans, flips it 100 times and records the number of heads he received (from the coin, not from anybody else). Then he calculates the probability of the coin turning up heads p = (number of heads) / 100.

Bravo!! He has just conducted the famous Bernoulli trials. Everybody is excited. Hundreds of years after the first trials, the methodology he laid out is just perfect.


Then he does it again, and again, and again. After some hours, he has done thousands of those. Everybody is pleased with his consistency, the coins always land on the table, head or tail.



Just before lunch time, he calculates the symmetric confidence interval for his experiment. 



In the afternoon, he repeats the experiments many times (he did more after lunch, because not a morning bird he is, and the jeans are tight as I told you).


He came to the conclusion that, 95% of the time, the true probability of turning up heads is in the confidence interval he calculated, and thus, he is 95% confident that each interval will contain the true probability of landing heads.


That was just through the roof. Everybody clapped and cheered. KDnuggets and Towards Data Science jumped right into the lab and recorded the results. The museum down the road wants the coin on their wall.


-- 


Then Scott in the corner asked him: "Hey Vuong, what's the probability of the true head-landing-rate being in the range of [0.499,0.501]? In the end, the coin is a physical object, it has a fixed head-landing-rate. amma right ? "


Vuong, proud Frequentist that he is, is pissed off. He knows that there is no philosophically sound answer to that question. For him, the confidence interval is interpreted over an infinite number of experiments, which he doesn't have time to conduct.




Vuong looks tense now. He is embarrassed in front of everybody, especially the starry-eyed girls.



Jumping to the side, Vuong pulled out a giant Sodium block, the size of a plate, that he always stores in the lab fridge, and tossed it into water.

BAM!!!! The lab explodes! Everybody crawls out. Smoke is all over the place. Told you it's dangerous experimenting with Vuong.
