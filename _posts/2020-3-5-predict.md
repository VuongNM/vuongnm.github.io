Predictions are hard, especially about the future. So when someone hands you a forecasting task, the first question to ask is: can this thing be predicted at all?

## What makes something predictable

Predicting means spotting patterns in what you've seen before. That only works if something connects the future to the past, or if some ground rule (like the laws of physics) drives the process. If neither is true, there's no point forecasting it.

Predicting something that is unpredictable in principle is just fortune telling. Sometimes it hits, sometimes it misses. (By the way, &lt;bitchy&gt; I'm a Libra everybody, so you know I hate being alone &lt;/bitchy&gt;. Btw btw, that's a prime-time false prediction, shame on you Cosmopolitan.)

In that situation, sophisticated models and metrics are no better than tarot cards and plasma globes. They're there to fool you. And if someone tries to sell you predictions like that, they're either possessed by something (ghosts, or even scarier, money) or they think you're dumb. Or both.

## When is it worth trying?

If there's an underlying principle behind the process, maybe. That could be physics, another law of nature, or a distributional law. Even then it can be hard: the mechanics of particles moving in a liquid are well understood, and you still can't predict where each one ends up.

So how good a prediction can get depends on the scale and scope of the problem, and on how much the thing you're predicting varies. And "good" is subjective. It's relative to what you expected in the first place.
