When you have to pick a tool, a library or a framework, there's a simple rule of thumb that saves a lot of headaches: the Lindy effect.

## What the Lindy effect says

For some things that don't wear out, the longer they have been around, the longer they are likely to stay around. Their remaining life expectancy is proportional to their age. People also call it Lindy's law.

It doesn't work for things with physical limits, like living beings or machines. A 90 year old person is not going to outlive a 20 year old. But it works well for ideas, religions and technologies.

The name comes from Lindy's, a restaurant in New York where comedians used to hang out and guess how long each other's shows would last. Their rule of thumb: the longer a show had already been running, the longer it would keep running. Across a whole collection of things like this, life expectancies follow a power law.

## Why it matters for tech choices

Look at how fast JavaScript frameworks come and go, then look at something *old* like the TCP protocol. TCP has been doing fine for generations in tech years, and it will most likely keep doing fine.

So when you have to choose, favor the older option. It has survived this long for a reason, it's more likely to be popular, and it gets more support and maintenance. That means fewer headaches for you.
