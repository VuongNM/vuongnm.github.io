Say you run a subscription service. Customers love your product: they sign up and renew every month. But not all of them. Some stop. How do you put a number on that, and know how sure you are about it?

## The model

The Beta-Geometric model is a simple way to describe this.

Start by assuming each customer cancels with a constant probability p every month. Then the chance that a customer cancels in exactly month k is

	P(cancel in month k) = (1 - p)^(k - 1) * p

They have to stay for k - 1 months, then leave. That's the Geometric distribution.

We don't know p, only that it's somewhere between 0 and 1. So we put a Beta distribution over it, which also lets different customers have different churn rates. Put the two together and you've got the Beta-Geometric model of churn.

## Fitting it

The casual way is to estimate p from your data with maximum likelihood, like in [this tutorial](http://brucehardie.com/talks/intro_tut_art_16_HO.pdf).

But we're not so casual now, are we? We also want to know how uncertain the estimates are. In this [notebook](https://github.com/VuongNM/study/blob/main/Beta%20Geometric%20Churn%20with%20censored%20data.ipynb), I fit the model to a synthetic dataset, including customers who haven't churned yet (censored data), and show that it captures the business process well.

You don't just get a single churn rate out of it. You get the whole distribution of churn rates, you can track it over time, and you can use it to reason about how your customers churn.
